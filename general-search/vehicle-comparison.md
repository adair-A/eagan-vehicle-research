## Comparable-price conclusions

- Bedrock Mazda3 costs $900 less and has 62,100 fewer miles than the Brookdale Camry. Its smaller cabin is a tradeoff. This comparison favors the Mazda if its title, actual history and inspection are satisfactory.
- Midwest Prius c is $1,695 cheaper than the Dahl Prius and has 36,309 fewer miles. It is two years older, smaller, has disclosed paint deterioration and one reported accident. The Dahl provides a larger/newer platform, but CARFAX minor damage and coolant-exchanger risk matter. Neither is an exact trim/year/mileage comparable to the other.
- ONG's 2016 Prius c at $10,195 and 98,010 miles appears $1,905 cheaper than Midwest's 2014/98,235 example. Exact-VIN auction damage, title uncertainty and the seller's rebuilding business explain why the apparent discount cannot be treated as equivalent clean-history value.
- The $9,349 Alliance 2014 Camry Hybrid/169,727 versus $9,399 Wayzata 2012/149,811 shows that newer is not automatically better value. Alliance's history summary is more readable; Wayzata has lower mileage and a one-owner badge. Battery/brake records would decide.
- Avalon 2014/140,004 costs $4,800 above the Alliance Camry Hybrid/169,727 for 29,723 fewer miles and more equipment/space, but has a reported accident. That premium is not compelling for minimum ownership cost.
- Corolla Hybrid 2023/113,449 at $16,999 is $2,004 above the Marshfield Prius 2021/136,074, with 22,625 fewer miles, newer safety technology and no damage reported in its available summary. That supports the stretch case; full history and recall checks are still required.
- Cheap 2013 Mazda3/77,193 at $10,349 is not a fair clean-history comparable: AutoCheck documents severe collision/airbag deployment plus a second collision.

These are advertised comparisons, not transaction prices or formal appraisals. Matching year, trim, mileage, history and condition exactly was not possible in the verified pool. No unsupported thousands-below-market claim is made. CarGurus model estimates are labeled algorithmic estimates, not independent proof.

## Negotiation approach

The evidence supports asking to honor the lowest currently visible fee-inclusive public offer, not inventing a universal 10% discount. For Bedrock, $12,349 before government fees is the concrete baseline. For Midwest, reconcile $11,900 dealer base with $12,100 CarGurus fee-inclusive. For Dahl, reconcile $13,797 CarGurus with $13,398 base + $399 fee and differing stale feeds. Request documented inspection repairs or their itemized cost as concessions. Any lower offer should follow an actual PPI estimate, tire/brake measurements or verified history discrepancy. No exact below-asking negotiation target can be substantiated from sufficiently matched transaction comparables.
